# 🏛️ Existing Solution Overview

India already has several government mechanisms to manage tomato price volatility. These interventions work at different stages — **controlling consumer prices during shortages, supporting farmers during price crashes, improving storage and transport, and using technology to reduce wastage.**

---

## 🔎 System 1 — When Prices Spike: Protecting Consumers

When weather events or crop diseases create shortages, tomato prices can rise sharply. The government responds by increasing supply in high-price markets and selling tomatoes to consumers at subsidised prices.

### Price Stabilisation Fund (PSF)

The **Price Stabilisation Fund (PSF)** provides financial support for managing price volatility.

- Government agencies such as **NCCF** and **NAFED** can procure tomatoes directly from surplus-producing markets.
- Tomatoes are transported from surplus states such as Karnataka and Maharashtra to high-price markets such as Delhi-NCR.
- Government agencies can then sell tomatoes through mobile vans and retail outlets at subsidised prices.
- During major price spikes, tomatoes have been sold at prices significantly below the open-market rate.

**Goal:** Increase supply in high-price markets and protect consumers from sudden price increases.

---

## 📊 System 2 — When Prices Crash: Supporting Farmers

When bumper production floods the market, tomato prices can fall sharply, sometimes below the level needed for farmers to recover their costs.

### Market Intervention Scheme (MIS)

The **Market Intervention Scheme (MIS)** is designed to support farmers growing perishable agricultural and horticultural commodities when market prices collapse.

- Government agencies can procure produce from markets experiencing severe price declines.
- The government provides support for transportation and storage under approved interventions.
- Produce can be moved from surplus regions to markets where demand is stronger.
- This helps reduce local oversupply and provides an alternative market for farmers.

**Goal:** Reduce the impact of extreme price crashes and prevent farmers from being forced to sell at distress prices.

---

## 💡 System 3 — The Long-Term Fix: Operation Greens

**Operation Greens** was originally launched for **TOP — Tomatoes, Onions and Potatoes** and aims to improve the overall value chain.

The programme focuses on reducing the gap between production and consumption centres through better infrastructure.

### Key interventions

1. **Transportation**
   - Financial support can help move produce from surplus-producing areas to deficit markets.

2. **Storage and Cold Chain**
   - Financial assistance supports the development of storage and cold-chain infrastructure.
   - Better storage gives farmers and traders more flexibility instead of forcing them to sell immediately during a price crash.

3. **Value Addition**
   - Processing facilities can convert surplus tomatoes into products such as puree and paste.
   - This reduces wastage and creates alternative markets for excess production.

**Goal:** Reduce supply-chain inefficiencies and make tomato prices less vulnerable to sudden supply shocks.

---

## 📈 System 4 — Technology and Innovation

The government has also focused on technology-based solutions through the **Tomato Grand Challenge Hackathon**.

### The approach

- The Department of Consumer Affairs and Ministry of Education invited ideas to improve the tomato value chain.
- More than **1,300 ideas** were received from startups, researchers and innovators.
- **28 projects were selected and funded**.
- These projects focus on areas such as:
  - Market demand forecasting
  - Better packaging and shelf-life extension
  - Reducing post-harvest losses
  - Tomato processing and value addition
  - Better supply-chain management

**Goal:** Use technology and innovation to reduce wastage, improve market efficiency and make the tomato supply chain more resilient.

---

## 🎯 Overall Government Approach

The existing system can be viewed as four layers:

| Situation | Government Response | Main Objective |
|---|---|---|
| **Prices spike** | PSF + NCCF/NAFED procurement and subsidised sales | Protect consumers |
| **Prices crash** | Market Intervention Scheme | Support farmers |
| **Structural supply problems** | Operation Greens | Improve storage, transport and processing |
| **Long-term innovation** | Tomato Grand Challenge | Develop technology-based solutions |

### The Gap

These programmes address important parts of the problem, but they are largely **reactive** — intervention often happens after prices have already moved sharply.

Our analysis suggests an opportunity for a more **predictive approach**: use historical price, arrival, seasonality and market-level data to identify early warning signals **before** a major price spike or crash occurs.
